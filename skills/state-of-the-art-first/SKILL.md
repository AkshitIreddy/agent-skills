# Research the technique that matters

Identify the decision current evidence could change: quality, compatibility,
latency, cost, or a known failure mode. Respect the user's chosen tools and
constraints. Routine edits using an established technique need no new survey.

Start with current primary documentation, maintained implementations, and
measurements for the relevant workload. Add independent comparisons or research
when sources disagree or the decision has substantial consequences. Read the
material supporting each claim; search snippets are leads, not evidence.

Use enough relevant sources to resolve the decision, without a fixed quota.
Expand an explicit broad research brief accordingly. Stop when further sources
do not change viable choices, trade-offs, or remaining uncertainty. Keep private
project information out of public queries.

Compare candidates under the project's actual hardware, data, and output
requirements. A newer model or additional processing stage is a candidate, not
an automatic improvement. Distinguish measured results from vendor claims.

For a reversible craft choice, build a small comparison early while filling
relevant evidence gaps. Settle expensive or hard-to-reverse dependencies before
committing to them. Research should inform the build, not indefinitely delay it.

Record the selected technique, supporting links, trade-offs, and limitations
where the project maintains decisions. Verify the claimed benefit on a
representative result against the existing or simpler baseline. Reuse that
evidence until a new requirement or version invalidates it.
